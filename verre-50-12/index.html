Verre 50 mm f/1.2 for full-frame mirrorless

Ten pieces of glass. One point of focus.

A fast standard prime, ground and assembled by hand in Annecy. Scroll to take it apart.

Pre-order the 50/1.2Try the aperture

Built like an instrument.

The barrel is milled from a single block of aluminium, anodised twice, and weighs 780 g. The focus ring turns 270 degrees end to end.

Open it up.

Inside, ten elements in seven groups. Each one is centred by hand to within two microns before the barrel is closed.

Every element has a job.

Two aspherical elements keep f/1.2 sharp to the corners. One low dispersion element stops colour fringing on bright edges.

All of it for one point.

Light enters at 72 mm across and leaves as a single point on the sensor, 18 mm behind the mount.

Thirteen blades, round at every stop.

Most lenses turn out-of-focus highlights into polygons as you stop down. Thirteen curved blades keep them round from f/1.2 to f/4. Try it on a portrait made with the lens.
