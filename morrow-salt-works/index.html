Reading the tide at Scarra Point

A salt works that only opens when the sea steps back.

Twice a day the water leaves the flats at Scarra Point for about four hours. That's our working window. The rakes go out, the pans are tended, and when the tide turns we go home.

It makes us slow and it makes us small. Around nine tonnes a year, every tin dated with the tide it was raked on.

Four days from seawater to flake.

No vacuum pans and no anti-caking agents. Water from the spring tide, time, and a fire kept just below a simmer.

1

Flood

On the spring tide we lift the sluice and let clean water run into the settling ponds.

Twice a monthon the biggest tides only

2

Settle

Two days of stillness. Sand and silt drop to the floor and the brine turns clear as window glass.

48 hoursundisturbed

3

Warm

The brine is held just under a simmer in shallow iron pans until hollow pyramids form on the surface.

About 16 hoursper pan, never boiled

4

Rake and dry

Flakes are drawn off by hand at low water and dried on linen racks in the sluice house for three days.
